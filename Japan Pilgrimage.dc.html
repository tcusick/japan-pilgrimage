The journey

Two pilgrimages: one in the temples, one on the trail.

Part one is a formal pilgrimage with the Zen center, led by the roshi and based in Kyoto from February 24 to March 12. It covers Kyoto’s great temples, with day trips to Nara, Kamakura, Shoin-ji, Eihei-ji and Koyasan.

Part two begins when the group departs. A train south to Kii-Tanabe, then on foot along the Nakahechi route of the Kumano Kodo from Takijiri to Chikatsuyu and Hongu. After a rest day at Yunomine Onsen, the Kumano River boat leads to Shingu and Hayatama Taisha, and the pilgrimage ends at Nachi. A night on Nachisan and an onsen day in Kii-Katsuura follow, with a stop at Hana-no-Iwaya and Onigajo on the way to Ise Jingu, before flying home from Kansai on March 23.

Thursday, March 11 · Free day · Tentative

Hozugawa river boat and Arashiyama

A slow day between the group pilgrimage and the Kumano Kodo: one train, one boat ride down the Hozu gorge, a long lunch and a walk. Nothing is booked, and everything after the boat is optional.

~9:30 a.m.JR Sagano Line from Kyoto Station to Kameoka, 20 to 30 min, $3. Walk about 8 min to the boat pier.
11:00 a.m.Boat from Kameoka to Arashiyama, 16 km, 100 min to 2 hr, $40 per adult. Buy tickets at the pier. Boats leave hourly on weekdays from 9:00 a.m. and the regular season starts March 10. There is no toilet or heating on board, so dress warmly.
~1:00 p.m.Land near Togetsukyo Bridge. Lunch in Arashiyama. Shigetsu, the shojin ryori restaurant at Tenryu-ji, serves vegetarian temple meals; reserve ahead and check prices.
AfternoonWalk the bamboo grove (free) and the riverside, or rest. Tenryu-ji is next to the landing, but the group visited it on March 7.
EveningJR from Saga-Arashiyama to Kyoto, 17 min, $2.

Times and fares are from 2026 listings and are not confirmed for 2027. Option: take the Sagano Romantic Train ($6, 25 min) to Torokko Kameoka first and ride a later boat. It runs March 1 to December 29 and is mostly closed Wednesdays.

On foot to the sea

Eleven nights on your own after the group departs: four short days on foot, a rest day, the river boat to Shingu, the finish at Nachi, a day at the Kii-Katsuura onsen, a stop at the Kumano coast, then Ise Jingu before the flight home.

Day 2 · Saturday, March 13 · Pilgrimage day 1

Takijiri-oji → Takahara

A short first day. Take the bus from Kii-Tanabe to Takijiri-oji, the traditional entrance to the mountains, and climb steeply to the ridge village of Takahara. The rest of the day is for the village and the view over the Hatenashi range.

Along the way
Takijiri-ojiOne of the five major oji shrines. Pilgrims purified themselves in the river here before entering the mountains. Just above the shrine is Tainai-kuguri, a narrow rock passage said to grant rebirth to those who crawl through.
TakaharaA hilltop farming village known for morning mist over the Hatenashi range. Takahara Kumano-jinja is among the oldest shrine buildings on the route.

Day 3 · Sunday, March 14 · Pilgrimage day 2

Takahara → Chikatsuyu

A shorter walking day. Follow the forest ridges past Gyuba-doji and drop into the Chikatsuyu valley, where you stay overnight. Chikatsuyu has more lodging than the Nonaka area, so the night is easier to book.

Along the way
TakaharaStart of the day. A hilltop farming village known for morning mist over the Hatenashi range.
Gyuba-dojiA small stone statue of a boy riding an ox and a horse at once, linked to the retired Emperor Kazan’s pilgrimage in the 10th century.
ChikatsuyuA village on the Hiki River where the trail meets the valley. Chikatsuyu-oji stands near the bridge. The Coop is the only place to buy food, so carry a lunch box.

Day 4 · Monday, March 15 · Sacred approach

Chikatsuyu → Hosshinmon-oji → Hongu → Yunomine

A short morning walk from Chikatsuyu to Tsugizakura-oji, so everyone sees the shrine, the cedars and the Nonaka-no-shimizu spring. A bus or road transfer then skips the long mountain section over Kobiro-toge and drops you at Hosshinmon-oji, the “gate of awakening.”

From there the walkers continue to Kumano Hongu Taisha, the first of the three Grand Shrines, and on to Oyunohara. A bus carries everyone to Yunomine, so there is no backtracking to Hosshinmon-oji the next morning.

Along the way
Tsugizakura-ojiA shrine ringed by giant cedars, centuries old, whose branches all reach south toward Nachi.
Nonaka-no-shimizuA spring below the shrine, listed among Japan’s 100 best waters.
Hosshinmon-ojiThe “gate of awakening,” once the outer torii of Hongu’s sacred precinct.
Hongu TaishaHead shrine of the more than 3,000 Kumano shrines across Japan. It moved to its present hill after the flood of 1889.
OyunoharaThe original shrine site on a river sandbank, marked by a great torii about 111 ft (34 m) tall.

Day 6 · Wednesday, March 17 · Kumano River

Yunomine → river boat → Shingu

Take a bus from Yunomine toward the Kawabune Center at Michi-no-Eki Kumano-gawa. The seated boat ride runs about 90 minutes along the route pilgrims once took by water, and lands near Kumano Hayatama Taisha, the third Grand Shrine. You sleep in Shingu.

Booked: 2:30 p.m. departure for 5 adults, $37 each ($183 total), booking no. 2802. Tours run in light rain, so bring rain gear. Heavy rain or high or low water can cancel the tour, so check email before leaving Yunomine.

Along the way
Kawabune CenterBoarding point for the river boat, reached by bus. Arrive 30 minutes early.

Non-walkersSame bus and river route, and the Hayatama Taisha visit. Kamikura can be skipped.

Day 7 · Thursday, March 18 · Pilgrimage finish

Shingu → Daimon-zaka → Nachi Taisha

Take the train and bus toward Nachi and get off for Daimon-zaka, the formal stone approach of about 0.6 mi (1 km). Then visit Kumano Nachi Taisha, Seiganto-ji and Nachi Falls, Japan’s tallest waterfall at 133 m. This is the formal end of the pilgrimage.

Along the way
Daimon-zakaA stone-paved approach lined with old cedars, leading up to the shrine.
Nachi Taisha & Seiganto-jiA Grand Shrine and the first temple of the 33-temple Saigoku Kannon pilgrimage, side by side above the falls.
Nachi FallsA 436 ft (133 m) single drop, worshipped as a deity in its own right.
Advanced optionContinue above Nachi toward Amida-ji and Mount Myohozan via Kakenuke-michi, then return.
Non-walkersBus farther uphill to Nachisan and meet the walkers at the sacred complex. Little hiking needed.

Day 9 · Saturday, March 20 · Kumano coast

Onigajo and Hana-no-Iwaya by e-bike

A morning train up the coast to Kumano-shi, then a day on e-bikes between two World Heritage sites on the Kumano coast: the sea cliffs of Onigajo and Hana-no-Iwaya, a shrine said to be the oldest in Japan, with no building, only a 45 m sacred rock beside the sea. An early-afternoon train carries you to Ise.

Options depending on how everyone feels. Kamenoi’s baths are open 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m. before check-out, so a later train gives more onsen time. The 12:25 p.m. Nanki from Kii-Katsuura leaves a full morning in the baths and reaches Ise about 5:30 p.m., but skips most of the coast. Hotel Urashima’s cave bath is open to non-guests after check-out. Anyone who would rather soak can take that train while the others ride. Times are estimates.

Days 9–12 · Sat–Tue, March 20–23

Ise Jingu → Osaka → home

Ise Jingu is the most sacred site in Shinto. The Inner Shrine (Naiku) enshrines Amaterasu, the sun goddess, and the Outer Shrine (Geku) enshrines Toyouke, goddess of food and harvest. By tradition you visit the Outer Shrine first. The main halls are rebuilt in plain cypress every 20 years; the current buildings date from 2013.

The rail route from Kii-Katsuura runs north along the coast, and the Kintetsu line from Ise goes straight to Osaka, so the trip ends without backtracking. Anyone leaving instead can take the JR Kuroshio from Kii-Katsuura toward Osaka and Kyoto; the 6:45 a.m. Kuroshio 12 reaches Kyoto at 11:17 a.m. (about $58 reserved, November 2026 timetable). The Nanki limited express to Ise runs only a few times a day, so check the 2027 timetable.

Along the way
GekuThe Outer Shrine, a short walk from Iseshi Station, set in old cedar forest.
Uji BridgeThe wooden bridge over the Isuzu River into the Inner Shrine, rebuilt with each 20-year renewal.
Isuzu RiverVisitors rinse their hands at the stone steps of the river before approaching the main sanctuary.
Okage YokochoA street of Edo-style shops and teahouses beside the Inner Shrine.

Before you go

Trip essentials

March weather

Typical highs 55–61°F (13–16°C), lows near freezing in Nara and the mountains, milder on the coast. Expect rain on some days; mountain mornings can be frosty.

Daylight

About 12 hours. Sunrise around 6:10 a.m., sunset around 6:10 p.m. On the 7–9 hour days, start by 8 a.m.

Holiday travel

Vernal Equinox Day falls on Sunday, March 21, 2027, and Monday the 22nd is a substitute holiday. Reserve Ise and Osaka lodging and the Kii-Katsuura to Ise and Ise to Osaka seats early.

Luggage

Mahoroma moves the bags the same day between lodgings through Shingu on March 17, and bags can be carried on the bus and train from Nachisan on. Walk with a day pack: water, lunch, rain shell, warm layer, headlamp. Large suitcases go ahead from Kyoto to the Osaka hotel by takkyubin before March 12, and are collected there on March 22.

On the trail

Numbered posts mark the route every 500 m. Mountain sections have few shops and limited mobile signal. Stone steps get slick when wet; poles help on the descents.

Money & food

Carry yen. Many minshuku and rural buses take cash only. Confirm vegetarian meals in writing with each lodging, since dashi is usually made with fish.
